# Swipe File: seven emails that booked calls

> Read before writing any sequence. Real sends that produced meetings and closed clients across seven different niches. Copy the chain of reasoning, not the wording - it's identical in all seven despite completely different products.

## 1. The shared DNA

**A. The product is named before the ask.** By line two or three the prospect knows the company and what it sells. There is not one pure pain question with no product among them.

**B. There's an explicit reason for the email.** "Asking because we build..." · "We just launched a creative studio tool..." · "Noticed your role in creative/marketing and wanted to show you this." Relevance is stated, never left to be inferred.

**C. What sits behind the yes is worthless without the sender.** A walkthrough of the vendor's method, a sample of the vendor's output, ideas framed as *how we could help*, a trial, or nothing at all. None can be extracted and reused. **This is the mechanism that keeps freebie hunters out - not tone, not qualification questions.**

**D. Proof is specific, or the sample is the proof.** 65K · Starbucks · 100x · 24% · 1M views in 90 days. Where there's no proof, the sample carries it and the email says plainly they just launched.

**E. The ask is a one-word yes about appetite.** Nobody asks for 15 minutes. Nobody asks for a calendar slot.

**F. Pain is a setup line, never the ask.** Three of them open on pain. None of them *ask* about pain.

**G. Four to six lines.** One idea, one ask.

**H. Two hand over a graceful out.** "If this isn't of interest, just reply 'no' and we'll take our time elsewhere." Cleaner yeses, faster nos.

## 2. The seven, torn down

### 1: Capacity ask + mechanism walkthrough *(outbound agency → AI hiring platform)*

> Subject: Clients on autopilot, {FIRST_NAME}
>
> Hi Priyanka,
> Can you take 2-3 new clients every month who need an agentic ai hiring platform?
> Asking because we build profitable outbound marketing channels (cold emails + LinkedIn + WhatsApp) for businesses like yours.
> I recorded a Loom video walking through exactly how it works.
> Want me to send it over?

| Line | Job |
|---|---|
| "Can you take {N} more {their customer type} every month?" | **capacity ask** - presupposes the outcome; yes = declaration of demand |
| "Asking because we {mechanism} for businesses like yours" | **identity + mechanism + reason for the email** |
| "I recorded a Loom walking through exactly how it works" | **the vehicle** - already made, and about the *method* |
| "Want me to send it over?" | **one-word ask** |

The capacity question flips the frame: not *do you need help* (invites advice-seekers) but *can you absorb what I'd bring* (only buyers say yes). Naming their exact product category - "who need an agentic ai hiring platform" - is what stops it reading as a template.

### 2: Made sample, no proof needed *(AI photoshoot studio → apparel brand)*

> Subject: sample shoot for Rains INT
>
> Hi Line, mind if I shared a sample AI photoshoot of some of the products from Rains INT
> We just launched a creative studio tool - an AI photoshoot and creative studio that helps clothing and apparel brands cut out costly studio shoots and production delays
> I can send the concept if you're curious

The cleanest sample-as-vehicle example. A photoshoot of *their* products has no standalone value - they can't produce shot two. Liking it is functionally identical to wanting the service. **No proof exists and the email says so.** Don't manufacture case studies; use this pattern instead.

### 3: Personal-result proof + walkthrough *(personal branding → coach)*

> Subject: Strategy for Ani
>
> Ani,
> While helping clients transform their lives, it can be tough to find time to steadily produce content that engages more people.
> I've built my own Instagram to 65K by concentrating on strategies that demand minimal time.
> So, I recorded a video showing How I can help you build a following that gets you Inbound leads taking just 4hrs/month of your time.
> Mind if I send the video over?

"A video showing how I can help you" is unambiguously a pitch, and they say yes anyway - that's the point. Anyone who accepts has agreed to be sold to. The constraint ("just 4hrs/month") pre-handles the real objection inside the outcome.

### 4: Conditional transaction *(private equity → business owner)*

> Subject: {{companyName}} exit options
>
> Hey Gobigmx,
> If I had buyers targeting businesses in your space and Go Big MX aligns with what they were after, would you be interested in selling if the details were right?

Two lines, no asset, no proof, and it outperforms a paragraph of pain questions. The hypothetical costs nothing to answer; "if the details were right" pre-handles terms. A yes to "would you sell" is as close to a buying signal as cold email produces. **The highest-intent form in this file.**

### 5: Noticed gap + ideas framed as our approach *(TikTok organic → DTC brand)*

> Subject: free tiktok idea
>
> Was on your website and thought your product would be a hit on TikTok - but saw you didn't have much traction there yet.
> We've been helping DTC ecom marketing teams generate 1M views in 90 days using TikTok organic, without spending a cent on paid ads.
> Had some viral video ideas for your team I wanted to share on how we could help.
> Mind if I send something over?
> P.S. if this isn't of interest, just reply "no" and we'll take our time elsewhere :)

**The five words that matter: "on how we could help."** Without them this is a detachable idea list that attracts collectors. With them, the yes is a yes to seeing the vendor's approach. If you use the ideas vehicle, that framing is mandatory.

### 6: Comparison explainer + product-interest ask *(AI presentations → marketing lead)*

> Hello Angela,
> Noticed your role in creative/marketing and wanted to show you this.
> We've helped the branding team at Starbucks these last few years to create presentations and visual pieces 100x faster using our AI presentation platform.
> It's sort of like if PowerPoint and ChatGPT came together to help you build stunning decks in minutes, save 1000+ hours every year, and tell your brand story better.
> Does this sound like something you might be interested in at all?
> P.S. if this isn't of interest to you, just reply "no" and we'll take a hike :)

No asset, nothing to collect - the only thing available to say yes to is the product. Everyone who replies positively is product-interested by construction. **Use the comparison explainer whenever the category is new**; one line beats a paragraph of description.

### 7: Blind spot + doubt case + trial *(attribution SaaS → marketing lead)*

> Hi [prospect],
> [Company] published 8 blog posts this month - how are you tracking the revenue they drive?
> [Case Study Name] thought they were doing it properly with UTMs, but found out they were under-reporting by 24% after using our tool.
> Can I give you a trial (no strings attached?)
> PS - saw your LinkedIn post about remote work vs WFH. Remote all the way!

**The doubt case is the strongest single structure here:** *{peer} thought they had this handled with {the obvious method}, then found {specific bad number} once they {used the product}.* It manufactures uncertainty only the product resolves.

The trial works because setup takes real effort and reveals their own data. **Trials only qualify when using them costs something.**

## 3. The skeletons

**S1 - Capacity + walkthrough** *(agencies, done-for-you, anything selling more customers)*
```
Can you take on {N} more {their customer type} {timeframe}?
Asking because we {mechanism, named concretely} for {businesses like theirs}.
I recorded a short video walking through exactly how it works.
Want me to send it over?
```

**S2 - Made sample** *(creative, content, design, media, anything with a visible output)*
```
Mind if I sent over a sample {deliverable} of {their specific product/page/asset}?
We {just launched / run} {company} - {what it is} that helps {ICP} {kill the cost or delay}.
Happy to send the concept if you're curious.
```

**S3 - Conditional transaction** *(discrete outcomes: selling, switching, replacing, partnering)*
```
If I had {the demand/asset/access they want} and {company} fit what they were after,
would you be interested in {the transaction} if the details were right?
```

**S4 - Blind spot + doubt case + trial** *(software, tools, anything measurable)*
```
{Observed specific fact} - how are you {tracking/handling} {the thing}?
{Named peer} thought they had it covered with {obvious method}, and found they were
{specific bad number} off once they {used the product}.
Can I give you a trial, no strings attached?
```

**S5 - Micro-POC (info-request)** *(complex, configurable, or POC-driven categories)*
```
Tell me the one {rule / process / workflow} that gives your team the most trouble - 
and we'll build it in {product} and send you a recording of it running.
We're {what the product is}, used by {ICP}. {Named client} runs on us after years of {old way}.
Takes us {timeframe} and costs you nothing. Want to try it?
```

**S6 - Explanation gap + walkthrough** *(when the buyer's pain is visibility, not capability)*
```
Most {problems} aren't {calculation/capability} problems. They're explanation problems - 
{the thing is usually right}, but showing how it was derived takes days.
{Company} is {what it is} for {ICP}. {Feature} traces {the thing} back to {the source}, in plain English.
I recorded a short video of it working through a live case. Want me to send it over?
```

**S7 - Peer walkthrough** *(the threaded Email-2 slot)*
```
The closest case to your setup is {named client} - {one line on who they are and their old way}.
They moved onto {product} and typically {hedged result}, with {the detail that proves control stayed with them}.
I recorded a walkthrough of how their setup was built and what the first few weeks looked like.
Want me to send it?
```

Mix freely with the **product-interest close** ("does this sound like something you'd be interested in at all?") and the **graceful-out P.S.**

## 4. Subject-line patterns

Three to five words. Lowercase or sentence case. Names their company or their outcome - never the sender's.

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| Their company + the topic | `{{company}} exit options` · `plan changes at {{company}}` · `{{company}} on mobile` |
| The deliverable, for them | `sample shoot for {{company}}` · `one rule, built for you` · `strategy for {{first_name}}` |
| The outcome | `clients on autopilot, {{first_name}}` · `more orders for {{company}}` |
| The blind spot | `agent payout disputes` · `payouts from eight months ago` |

Avoid `$` amounts and shouty formatting. "Free" is a soft avoid, not a ban - "free tiktok idea" booked calls.

## 5. Before/after conversions

| Old style | Rebuilt | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| "are you still chasing invoices by hand every week?" | "can you take on 3-4 more retainer clients a quarter? asking because we run outbound for {niche} - {client} added {N} in {timeframe}. recorded a short video on how it works, want it?" | pain-ask → capacity ask; product named; proof added; walkthrough behind the yes |
| "found 3 things slowing your checkout - want them?" | "rebuilt your top product page the way we'd ship it and put it on a live link - want to see it next to your current one?" | findings (detachable) → hosted sample |
| "went through reviews of the tool you're running and pulled the gaps - want them?" | "recorded a short video showing what {product} does differently on the two things people complain about most - want it?" | competitor research they can act on alone → walkthrough |
| "tell me which modules you use and I'll put together a checklist - want me to?" | "pick the one process that drives your team maddest and I'll build it working so you can click through it - want to try?" | checklist (substitutes the work) → built pilot (samples it) |
| "how much is manual data entry costing you right now?" | "{peer} thought their entry errors were rounding noise, then found {N}% of orders needed rework. worth checking on your side?" | cost question → doubt case + product-interest ask |
| "worth a quick chat?" | "recorded a 4-minute walkthrough of how we'd do it on your setup - want me to send it?" | meeting ask → permission ask with a vehicle |

**Every row moves the ask from symptom to appetite, and moves what's behind the yes from something usable alone to something that only means anything with the sender attached.**

## 6. What none of them do

- None ask for time. No "got 15 minutes", no calendar links, no "quick chat".
- None hand over a document.
- None use the word audit, report, analysis, benchmark, or teardown.
- None open with the sender's credentials or a paragraph about the company.
- None stack two proof points.
- None run past six lines.
- None ask a question the prospect can answer without revealing whether they'd buy.
